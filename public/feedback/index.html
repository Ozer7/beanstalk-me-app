        How the 10 AI reviewers were made different

        • A persona grid, not a list of names. Each reviewer differs on several things at once: who they are to the child (parent, grandparent, teacher, stranger), what they know (illustrator, print shop, librarian, IT, nothing special), age, and how much they care.
        • A lens. Each is told the one thing they notice first: the face, the words, the print quality, the price, faithfulness to the tale, whether it's giftable. That spreads the critiques across the whole book instead of all landing on the same flaw.
        • A temperament and a survey style. Some are generous, some hard to please, and one is a tired crowd worker who answers in a few words, like real survey takers.
        • Independent calls. No reviewer sees another's answer, and each looks at the pages in a different order.
        • A measured check. Every answer is turned into an embedding. Any answer too similar to an earlier one (cosine similarity above 0.92) is redone once with a different lens. The similarity numbers are below, so "different" is measured, not hoped for.
